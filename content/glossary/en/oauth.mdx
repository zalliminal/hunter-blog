## What is OAuth?

In one sentence: OAuth lets you give a third-party app access to your data without giving it your password.

OAuth is an open standard for token-based authorization. Instead of sharing credentials, the user authorizes a client application to access specific resources on their behalf. The most common version is OAuth 2.0, which is used by Google, GitHub, Facebook, and most major platforms for "Login with..." functionality.

OAuth is not an authentication protocol itself — it's for authorization. OpenID Connect (OIDC) builds on OAuth 2.0 to add authentication.
